## 0. The finding

1. **The trap census is 24 rows; 20 are open; making all 20 unrepresentable costs machine words in exactly 4 of them.** The other 16 are compile-time rejections with a `<line>:<col>` (the A13/A15/C1 form) inside the planning scan `bebop.bp` already runs -- zero words, ~300 lines total (estimate). 2. **The bounds arithmetic is dominated by constant indices, and that is what makes proof-driven elimination cheap.** Of the 2,116 index sites in `bebop.bp`, 1,852 (87.5 %) have a literal index and 1,326 (62.7 %) are `[0]`; only 264 have a register index. Unconditional checks cost ~6,600 words = **+15.7 %** of bin_words 42,078; with a declared length on array parameters (`[i64; k]`, the cheapest dependent type there is) every literal-index check is discharged at zero words and the residual is 264 x 4 = **+2.5 %**, falling to ~**+0.7 %** if 70-80 % of register-indexed loops hoist (the CHOP/JVM priors). Section 6 gives both budgets.
3. **Lean 4 over Coq/Rocq, for one decisive reason under the certificate decision:** Lean 4's kernel has been re-implemented independently several times in 5-10k lines (lean4checker, nanoda, lean4lean -- from memory on sizes), which is the existence proof that a kernel re-checker in Bebop is a bounded task; and its `bv_decide` pipeline already produces exactly the LRAT certificate the Bebop checker consumes, so Lean and Bebop check the SAME artifact. Coq wins on extraction and on the Sail AArch64 model's maturity -- both matter for a verified compiler (Tier 3), which was not chosen; that door is what the Lean choice costs. Section 5.
4. **"Whole language" scoped by `typecheck.py`'s BUILTIN table is 35 builtins, and the table is already wrong by one:** `scan` (emitted by `emit_sys_scan`, modelled by bpref) is absent, so the scope is 36. 5. **The theory fragment is QF_ABV, not QF_LIA + arrays.** Every value is Z/2^64 with wrap, `*`, `/`, shifts; linear integer arithmetic is unsound for wrap unless every VC carries mod-2^64 side conditions, which are nonlinear. Section 7.

7. **Tier 4 as chosen is 3-5x the cost of the rejected refinement route for the same trap coverage, and the elaborator will be the largest program in the tree.** A kernel checker for a Lean-subset calculus in Bebop: 150-400 fns; an elaborator with unification: 200-400 fns; together they exceed the 511 cap on their own and cannot live inside `bebop.bp` (287 fns). There is also a design blocker the decision has not met yet: Bebop's `let` REBINDS a fn-scoped register (LANGUAGE.md:41-43) and loops rebind on back-edges, so a type that mentions a variable is unsound without per-binding renaming and loop invariants -- the problem Verus exists to solve for Rust, and ATS took a decade on. Section 8.
8. **The amended thesis draws a clean line:** a second pass is justified where its output is a REJECTION with a position (definite assignment, escape, types, bounds proofs, unresolved calls) or a CERTIFICATE (the fragment trace); it is not justified where its only output is a faster or smaller binary (A12's refutation stands untouched). Section 4.

## 2. Q1 -- the gap between 114 gates and a theorem (unchanged by the decisions; the decisions are its answer)

Differential testing against bpref establishes, per tested program and input, that `bebop.bin` and `bpref` printed the same i64 or trapped alike -- a finite conjunction with four holes: (i) the program space is the generator's grammar, which excludes the shapes LANGUAGE.md marks UNDEFINED (`:55`: "the fuzzer avoids that shape and so should you"); (ii) 25 of 36 builtins are `return 0` in the oracle; (iii) programs where bpref raises `IndexError` are adjudicated as the PROGRAM's bug (WORKER-CARD), so undefined shapes are excluded, not compared; (iv) the two implementations share one mental model (T125's non-short-circuit `&&`/`||` is a compiler accident that bpref mirrors rather than adjudicates). Mutation tests show each gate is sensitive to some change, not to all.

`gen3 == gen4` proves the compiler is a fixed point of itself -- stability, not correctness (Thompson, CACM 1984, cited; ROADMAP D5 says so). DDC needs a second implementation: `expr_compile.bp` cannot compile the surface; `bpref` cannot RUN `bebop.bp` because `cli_compile` reads through `sys_open`/`sys_slurp` which bpref stubs -- a 40-80 line gap (estimate), which under the decisions becomes the interim DDC witness while the Lean semantics is being built (the Lean semantics cannot run on the box; section 5.4).

The theorem the decisions aim at: for every program P the compiler accepts and every input, the AArch64 words of `compile(P)` behave as `[[P]]` in the mechanised semantics, where behaviour includes the trap set. What is missing is, in order: a normative scripted semantics (Tier 1), a language whose semantics is a FUNCTION (the trap census: no UNDEFINED reads), a machine semantics of the emitted subset, a per-binary validation connecting them, and a trust root that names its tools. Every following section costs one of these.

## 4. The amended thesis: where a second pass is and is not justified

The amendment is: compile time may be spent because an agent will wait; ambiguity and silent wrongness may not be tolerated because an agent will build on them; a second pass is permitted where it buys impossibility of error. The line this draws, applied to the tree:

- **Justified: a pass whose output is a rejection with a position.** Definite assignment (row 3), escape (rows 4, 5), live-symbol counts (row 7), type tags (rows 11, 14, 15), unresolved calls (row 17), and -- the expensive one -- bounds and division proofs (rows 1, 2, 10, 12). These are whole-function facts; `bebop.bp` already computes several in its planning scan (`collect_fns` publishes per-fn facts that `emit_bl` reads, bebop.bp:664-672 on 2026-09-09 -- the B1 comment above `fntab_fact_lookup`, `bebop.bp:712` at `97895d9`, re-derived 2026-09-13; `hoist_scan`; `loop_alloc_safe`), so "one pass" in this tree has always meant "the EMITTER has no IR", not "the source is read once".
- **Justified: a pass whose output is a certificate.** The fragment trace of Tier 2 (section 9) records `(pos, word range, window before/after)` at emission -- no second pass at all -- and the validator is a chain gate, not a compile step.
- **Not justified: a pass whose only output is a faster or smaller binary.** A12 (flat IR + graph RA) was refuted on its own numbers (0-2 % of K5 against >= 5 %) and nothing in Phase F revives it. LICM/SROA/CSE would make the bounds elimination of section 6 slightly better and are still not justified: the elimination that matters is by TYPE (declared lengths), not by dataflow.
- **Justified but bounded: dependent type checking.** Normalisation during checking is evaluation at compile time and can diverge; the checker must carry fuel and a gated compile-time number (`K5_typed` <= a committed multiple of K5). The amendment permits the cost; it does not permit an unbounded one, because an agent waiting forever is the same failure as a wrong answer.
- **Where the amendment stops.** It authorises passes, not words: a bounds check the compiler cannot discharge is still a word in a loop, and section 6 prices it. The thesis clause "no stack-machine words" survives; the clause added is "safety words are budgeted per construct (D11-F `word_budget.txt`) and discharged by proof or hoisted where the compiler can see the bound".

## 6. Memory safety in A8: `[T]` with length, checks eliminated by proof -- the word budget both ways

### 6.1 The layout and the check (after A5 step 1b every `[i64]` value is a cell index)

The length lives in the header cell at `data - 1`, written by `emit_zeros` and the four aggregate sites (each already ends with `sub x0,x0,x17 ; lsr x0,x0,#3`; +2 words and +1 cell per allocation). A `[T]` value stays ONE i64 (the data index) so the ABI (x0..x13, one register per value, 3-cell window entries) is untouched; a two-register fat value is refuted for that reason. The check on a register index: `sub xt,xbase,#1 ; ldr xl,[x17,xt,lsl #3] ; cmp xidx,xl ; b.hs trap84` = **4 words** (unsigned compare covers negatives); on a literal index k: `sub ; ldr ; cmp xl,#k ; b.ls trap84` = **3 words**; `[0]` keeps its single access word plus 3. `trap 84` is a new TRAPS.md row with the pc in the stub's text (~4 stub words). The store's own objects get the same check at library level (`st_get`/`st_put` against `st_len`, `store.bp:270-274,180`; ~10 lines, 0 compiler words) and `st_alloc` compares its cursor against the mapping size (3 lines).

### 6.2 The two budgets against bin_words 42,078

Textual sites in `bebop.bp` are used as the proxy for emitted accesses (the ratio is what matters; the WORD_DELTA of the 86 frozen constructs is the measurement):

| arm | literal-index sites (1,852) | register-index sites (264) | words | bin_words | K5 (est.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| (i) unconditional checks | 1,852 x 3 = 5,556 | 264 x 4 = 1,056 | **+6,612** | 48,690 = **+15.7 %** | +5-15 % (scanner loops are `pos[0]`/`n[0]`-bound and codegen-bound) |
| (ii) declared lengths on array parameters and locals (`[i64; k]`, `[i64; n]` with `n` a symbol): every literal index below a declared length is discharged at compile time | 0 for every site whose base has a declared length >= k; residual = sites on undeclared bases, target 0 | 264 x 4 = 1,056 | **+1,056** | 43,134 = **+2.5 %** | ~+1-3 % |
| (iii) (ii) + hoisting: loops whose every index is the loop counter and whose bound is compared against the declared length once at entry (+2 per loop, -4 per elided access); prior 70-80 % of register-indexed accesses (CHOP 1907.04241: 80.12 %; JVM 2403.02416: 69.8 % uncomplicated traversals -- abstracts) | 0 | ~60-80 residual x 4 = 240-320, plus ~50-100 loops x 2 | **+350-500** | ~42,500 = **+0.8-1.2 %** | ~0 |
| (iv) (iii) + Tier 4 index types (`Fin n`): the residual register indices carry their proof in the type | 0 | ~0 | **+0 at use sites; +2 per index creation** | ~42,200 = **+0.3 %** | ~0 |

Two facts make arm (ii) the load-bearing one and they are both counted, not assumed: 87.5 % of the compiler's index sites are literal, and 62.7 % are `[0]` -- the `pos[0]`, `n[0]`, `fntab[k]` idiom of a compiler that passes state through small arrays. A declared length on a PARAMETER (`pos: [i64; 2]`) is the smallest dependent type in the language and it alone removes ~84 % of the unconditional cost. The check that the caller's actual has that length is then a VC at the CALL site (or a runtime check there, once per call instead of once per access).

### 6.3 What the elimination needs from the rest of Phase F

- Arm (ii) needs A8's type tags (the `fntab[3837]` side channel of the A8 blueprint) extended with a length field: a compile-time fact per symbol, in the planning scan -- a justified second pass under section 4.
- Arm (iii) needs a counter-index recogniser; A3's `tools/lin_census.py` counts affine BODIES (bebop.bp: 144 loops, 0 affine), not counter-indexed ACCESSES, and repurposing it to count "loops whose every index is the loop counter" is ~1 day and is the pre-measurement that decides the 70-80 % prior. Nothing here needs an IR.
- Arm (iv) needs `tcheck.bp` (section 8).
- **Cell stepping must be decided first.** `typecheck.py:46-57` legalised `arr + k` on 2026-09-08 and bpref models it as `Cells(off+k, n-k)` -- a fat value with nowhere to live natively. Under a header cell, `(a + k)[i]`'s header is a data cell of `a`. Re-headering the view clobbers `a[k-1]`; a two-cell handle breaks the ABI. The only consistent option is to make stepping UNREPRESENTABLE (typecheck finding again; the `emit_sys_*` conversions already add the base inside the builtin) and to express stepped views as `(base, offset)` pairs. The store is unaffected: `st_get(base, obj, i) = base[obj + 2 + i]` is index arithmetic on an i64, bounded by `st_len`. First census: the number of stepping sites (not in any document; 14 corpus files were rewritten to it per the A5 row).

### 6.4 The gate

`bounds_census: checked + proven + hoisted = 100 % of index sites; unchecked_fn = <count>` printed by a script over the emitted trace (section 9) or, before the trace exists, by a static census over `bebop.bp` and the corpus; plus `bin_words` with a committed `word_budget.txt` line per construct, and the C1 §6.3 form `K6 <= 1.2x` with the HOISTED check on the sgraph2 frontier loop. Arm (i) is the first landing (it closes rows 1-2 immediately); arms (ii)-(iii) are the A8 gate proper.

## 7. The certificate checker in Bebop

### 7.1 Which theory, and why not QF_LIA + arrays

Every Bebop value is Z/2^64; `+ - *` wrap; `/` and `%` are signed truncating with `x/0 = 0`; shifts take the count mod 64; comparisons are signed. A VC that says `i < len` where `i = j + 1` is FALSE in the integers-with-wrap if `j = 2^63 - 1`, and QF_LIA cannot express that without a side condition `j + 1 < 2^63`, which itself is a wrap-free statement only if EVERY intermediate in the VC carries one; in general the encoding is `mod 2^64`, which is nonlinear (QF_NIA, undecidable in general). The fragment that is both sound and decidable is **QF_BV** for the scalar part. The array part -- the one heap array `M`, `select(M, a+i)`, `store(M, a+i, v)` -- is bounded per VC (a fragment touches a handful of cells), so the VC generator eliminates it by read-over-write case splits (McCarthy's axioms applied syntactically, cited) before anything is sent to a solver. What reaches the solver is QF_BV; the solver (or Lean's `bv_decide`) bit-blasts it to CNF and refutes the negated VC; the certificate is **LRAT**.

Where QF_LIA IS enough: index arithmetic under declared lengths is small (cell indices are < 2^29 in the reserve); the generator can emit a separate, trivially bit-blasted no-wrap side VC and then a linear VC whose unsatisfiability certificate is a set of Farkas coefficients -- a checker of ~5 fns. This is an optimisation with a cost: a second certificate format in the trusted checker. Recommended only after the LRAT path is green.

### 7.2 What the checker must contain, and its size

| module | job | fns (est.) | lines (est.) | anchor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| VC reader | parse the VC term (cells: op, a, b) and the binding "this certificate refutes the negation of VC #k of fn f" | 8-12 | 300-500 | `bebop.bp`'s own reader loops are ~20 lines per form |
| bit-blaster | 64-bit add/sub/and/or/xor/not, shifts by constant and by variable (barrel), eq/ult/slt, mux, mul-by-constant, general mul (shift-add, ~64 adders), sdiv/srem only if a VC needs them (rare; the generator can keep divisions symbolic) | 25-40 | 1,000-1,800 | an adder is ~10 lines; the barrel shifter and the multiplier dominate |
| Tseitin | AIG/gates -> CNF, variable numbering | 5-8 | 200-300 | |
| LRAT checker | clause DB (arrays), assignment array, per-lemma hinted unit propagation to conflict, deletion steps, final empty clause | 8-12 | 300-500 | the ACL2 checker of Heule/Hunt/Kaufmann/Wetzler (ITP 2017, cited) and cake_lpr (Tan/Heule/Myreen, TACAS 2021, cited) are small; `dpll.bp` (106 lines, 3 fns) shows the data structures in the language |
| **total** | | **45-65** | **1,700-2,900** | |

Against the compiler's 287 fns: the checker is a standalone program (`tcheck.bp` or `cert.bp`) compiled by `bebop.bin`, so it has its OWN 511-fn cap and does not share the compiler's. It must NOT be put inside `bebop.bp`: the compiler does not check certificates during compilation; the chain does, as a gate. Cost: 5-8 person-weeks (estimate), of which the bit-blaster is most.

### 7.3 What the certificate binds, and the negative gate

A certificate is worthless unless the checker also verifies that the CNF it refutes ENCODES the VC: that is why the bit-blaster is inside the trusted checker rather than in the solver. The gate that makes this falsifiable: `checker_neg: 0 accepted of N` where N corrupted certificates (a flipped literal, a deleted hint, a CNF for a different VC, a valid LRAT for a satisfiable VC) are fed to the checker -- the mutation discipline T123 already applies to gates, applied to the checker.

### 7.4 What remains trusted no matter what

1. `seed/seed.S` (1,480 bytes): loads and jumps; compiles nothing.
2. The checker's SOURCE (readable in full: ~2-3k lines) and its BINARY, which is produced by `bebop.bin` -- Thompson's attack applies to the checker exactly as to the compiler. Mitigation, not elimination: the checker is also executed under bpref (with the file builtins of section 2) on every certificate batch, a diverse second executor; and the checker is small enough to be re-checked by hand.
3. The VC GENERATOR: the code that turns a program + rule into an obligation (in `bebop.bp` for bounds/division/overflow, in the type checker for user theorems). This is the largest trusted piece and the operator's decision does not touch it; it should be the first thing Tier 2's fragment validation is turned on, and TRUST-CHAIN.md must say it is trusted.
4. The bit-blaster's definitions of the 64-bit operators: they ARE the checker's belief about what `+`, `<<`, `<` mean. They are cross-checked against the mechanised semantics's definitions by the conformance gate (the same constructs through both).
5. The LRAT format's soundness argument (published; the algorithm is ~100 lines and can be argued on paper).
6. The certificate-to-obligation binding (7.3).
7. The hardware and the Linux kernel, out of scope.

NOT trusted after the decision: Lean 4, its kernel and its compiler; CaDiCaL or any SMT solver; the elaborator (section 8). They are producers; a wrong producer yields a rejected certificate, never an accepted false one. That is the de Bruijn criterion and it is CakeML's own arrangement for cake_lpr.

## 8. Tier 4 -- dependent types directly in Bebop: the honest cost

### 8.1 What has to exist

A dependent type system needs (a) a KERNEL: terms, substitution, weak-head normalisation, conversion (definitional equality), universes or a fixed hierarchy, inductive types with eliminators (without at least `Nat`, `Bool`, arrays and equality as inductives no theorem about a program can be STATED); (b) an ELABORATOR: bidirectional checking of the surface syntax, implicit arguments, unification (at least first-order / pattern), coercions between `i64` and the index types, and error reporting with positions; (c) the ERASURE that leaves the compiler's emitter untouched.

Sizes, anchored:

| artifact | known implementations | Bebop size (est.; factor 2-3x for no closures/strings/hashmaps/exceptions, 25.6 lines/fn) |
|---|---|---|
| kernel for a Lean-subset calculus | nanoda (Rust), lean4checker, lean4lean: of the order of 5-10k lines each (from memory); a minimal MLTT without inductives ("tutorial" implementations) ~1-2k lines Haskell | 4-12k lines, **150-400 fns** |
| elaborator with unification, no tactics | 2-4k lines in an ML (minimal bidirectional + pattern unification, from memory of such implementations); Lean 4's real elaborator is >100k lines | 5-10k lines, **200-400 fns** |
| the certificate checker (section 7) | -- | 1,700-2,900 lines, 45-65 fns |
| `bebop.bp` today | -- | 7,356 lines, 287 fns |

Consequences that the decision must absorb:
- The kernel + elaborator (350-800 fns) exceed the 511-fn cap ON THEIR OWN and cannot be added to `bebop.bp` (287) under it. The cap has been raised once (256 -> 512, A1b); raising it again is a relayout of `fntab` zones, but a 1,000-fn compiler is not the project's shape.
- The design that fits: **two artifacts, one trusted.** `tcheck.bp` = the kernel checker + the LRAT/bit-blast module (the certificate checker of section 7 and the dependent type checker are the SAME trusted program: a proof term may close a goal by `bv_decide_cert(#n)`, which the kernel accepts iff the LRAT module accepts certificate n -- Lean's `bv_decide` is exactly this reflection step). The ELABORATOR is untrusted and standalone (`elab.bp`, or Lean itself elaborating Bebop's types into exported terms -- the cheapest route to a first theorem, since Lean's elaborator exists and its output is what `tcheck.bp` re-checks). `bebop.bp` gains only: parsing of type annotations and `theorem`/`requires`/`ensures` blocks as inert syntax, erased before emission, and the emission of VCs for rows 1, 2, 10, 12 into a side zone. The chain runs `elab` then `tcheck` as gates.
- Compile-time cost: `tcheck.bp` over the whole selfhost corpus (833 fns) is a chain gate; the amended thesis permits it; the gate `tcheck_ms` is a committed number so a divergent normalisation shows up as a number, not a hang.

### 8.2 The design blocker the decision has not met

Bebop's `let` REBINDS a fn-scoped register (LANGUAGE.md:41-43); a `while` rebinds on every back-edge. A dependent type `[i64; n]` that mentions `n` is unsound the moment `n` is rebound, unless (i) the checker renames every `let` (SSA-style, a justified second pass) and (ii) every loop carries an INVARIANT over the types of the variables it rebinds. (ii) is not a detail: it is the reason Verus (Lattuada et al., OOPSLA 2023, cited) and Dafny (Leino, LPAR 2010, cited) exist as separate designs from Agda/Idris -- dependent or refinement types over mutable state need loop invariants, and an agent has to write them. ATS (Xi) is the precedent for dependent types over an imperative core and it was a decade of work (from memory). F* does it through an effect system. None of them is small.

So the honest statement of Tier 4 as chosen: it is a research project whose first deliverable is a kernel checker (which the certificate decision needs anyway) and whose SURFACE will, in its first usable form, look like refinement types with invariants (`{i : i64 | i < len a}`, `invariant i <= n`) elaborated into dependent terms -- because that is what an imperative language can carry. The full dependent surface (types indexed by runtime values, `Fin n` indices, length-indexed matrices for B7's rank-n tensors) arrives after the kernel and the invariant machinery exist. The operator rejected "refinement types alone"; the argument here is that refinement-SHAPED annotations checked by a DEPENDENT kernel are the same decision executed in the only order that has a first theorem in under a year.

### 8.3 Cost (estimate)

Kernel checker in Bebop 12-20 weeks; elaborator (or Lean-as-elaborator + exporter, the cheaper path) 8-16 weeks; annotation syntax + erasure + VC emission in `bebop.bp` 2-3 weeks; first theorems (`fp_mul`, `isqrt`, the money laws) 3-5 weeks. This is 3-5x the refinement-only route (6-10 weeks) for the same trap coverage; it buys the ability to STATE theorems inside the language, which is the operator's product.

## 9. Tier 2 -- translation validation from the one-pass trace (unchanged, and it is where the certificate checker gets its first customer)

The emitters named in the brief show the shape: `emit_array_get` (bebop.bp:4136-4165 at `97895d9`, re-derived 2026-09-13; was 3937-3963) knows, at emission, the source position, the two operands' kinds and registers, the destination and the exact 1-2 words -- a complete local triple over the register model; `emit_bl` (:651-674 at `97895d9`; was :627-646) is 1 or 3 words chosen by a published fact; `emit_enum_ctor` (:586-625 at `97895d9`; was :562-599) is 11 straight-line words with an ordering constraint A6's defect violated; `emit_match_rt` (:2086-2144 at `97895d9`; was :1976-2025) is one diamond per arm with `patch_b` back-patching -- the arm boundaries (`pne`, `q`, `prevb`) are known at emission. So the emitter can record `(pos, first word, last word, window-before digest, window-after digest, patch list)` per construct into a side zone (A10's planned reloc zone) with no second pass: ~30 sites, ~50-100 lines, 0 code words, fixpoint untouched.

Gate: `tv_fragments: validated=N total=N` over the 86 constructs, then over the self-compile. Cost 6-10 weeks (estimate). What it buys the agent: a per-BINARY certificate ("this build is right"), which is the agentic need, without proving the compiler in general.

## 12. What can be proved here that cannot be proved in C or Rust -- and its limits (compressed; unchanged by the decisions)

Holds here and not there: one value type Z/2^64 with every operator total and NO floats (`fp.bp`'s Q32 is integer arithmetic: `fp_mul`'s identity and `isqrt`'s bracket are QF_BV statements, today tested on 1.2M values, provable by a quantifier); ONE heap array after A5 1b, so aliasing is interval disjointness with the bump allocator's monotone cursor as the invariant -- arithmetic, not separation logic (C needs provenance models and VeriFast/Frama-C/RefinedC; safe Rust has this via the borrow checker, RustBelt (Jung et al., POPL 2018, cited), and loses it at `unsafe`); first-order, monomorphic, static call graph of 287 fns in a table; capacity as loud traps in the theorem's alphabet ("v, or trap c in {...}") rather than C's third disjunct; and a language small enough (45 rules, 36 builtins) to specify COMPLETELY, which neither C nor Rust has.

Limits: until rows 1-2 are checks or VCs, every theorem carries "all indices in range" as an undischargeable universal hypothesis (section 6.1's argument: an OOB store is a legitimate `store(M, ...)` in the theory); 64-bit wrap is not Z, so money theorems carry no-overflow side conditions (row 12); the 26 syscalls, persistence and the 7 threading builtins are axioms, so no theorem crosses a clone or a crash (B1's torn-write harness stays a test); rows 3 and 22 are the two remaining non-array UNDEFINED reads; and a source theorem reaches the binary only through F4's validation. The defensible sentence is: every VC a Bebop program generates lies in one decidable theory (QF_ABV) with no floats, no provenance and no ownership logic, over a language whose whole semantics and whole compiler output can be checked by programs the project controls -- once F0-F2 are done. Before that the sentence is false.
