# Twelve mathematical mappings, tested against the engine that exists: what is decorative, what is load-bearing, and what mathematics would actually pay

## 0. Executive summary (10 lines, leading with what is REFUTED and with the decorative / load-bearing split)

1. **REFUTED outright, six of twelve: #4 martingale cardinality estimators, #6 Itô/quadratic-variation tier switching, #7 SDE completion-time prediction, #8 Fokker-Planck over degrees, #11 phase-portrait/Lyapunov/bifurcation analysis, and the L^p half of #12.** Each answers a question this engine does not ask: cardinalities are exact from `rp[k+1]-rp[k]` (B7:15, `gb.bp:376-378`), so there is no estimate for a martingale to de-bias; the "12 GB/s bifurcation" is at a number the DRAM row retracted -- one A78 streams at 2.86 GB/s, three at 28/28/28 ms with zero contention, and a gather never approaches the memory system at all (journal 852) -- so the saturation knee does not exist at P <= 3 for scans and does not exist for traversals; the diffusion term in #7 has no measured physical source and would measure the model's own misspecification; a degree histogram over `rp` is exact in O(n) (~3 ms at 1M), and evolving a PDE to approximate an exact 3 ms count is strictly worse; there is no bytecode and no interpreter for #11's second half to remove overhead from; and L^p answers an embedding workload the project has not got (W is an order log, ROADMAP B8; the arXiv corpus has no vectors).
2. **Decorative, not load-bearing, three: #2 filtrations, #5 the Itô integral, and the first half of #10.** "The store is a filtration" restates append-only + two superblocks + CoW `prev` (store.bp:1-14, mvcc.bp:3-15) and buys no theorem by itself; "delta batches as an Itô sum" is B4's tail COO + eWiseAdd at promotion (B4:23-36) and its "no reallocation" clause is contradicted by the measured G8 row until B4 step 1 lands (L0 rebuilt whole per batch, 747 ms stall, ROADMAP Measured); "citation graphs as CSR so SpMSpV runs at bus bandwidth without pointer chasing" IS `gb.bp` + `gen_gb.bp`, and its physics claim is false -- the gather is latency-bound at 133 ns against a 0.67 ns traffic floor (journal 852), the CSR gather IS a dependent-load chain, and every traversal win in the tree (E1 6.24x, D2's surviving +28 %) came from more misses in flight, not fewer bytes.
3. **Load-bearing, two and a half: the spectral gap (from #10, tying #3 and #9 together), a convergence-gated PageRank (from #9), and an MMU-enforced read-set fence (from #2).** The one number that bounds #3's walk length, #9's iteration count and whether #10's Fiedler ordering could beat BFS order under D3 is lambda_2 of the walk matrix. The tree ALREADY computes it, with Hotelling deflation, a spectral-gap switch and a Fiedler-sign bipartition -- at n <= 64 (`spectral.bp:6-14, :89`, `sgamma.bp:4-9`) -- and never over the store or at scale. `gb_pr` runs a FIXED 10 iterations by oracle law (`gb_pr.bp:4-5`); on the uniform sgraph2 graph that is about 10 bits of a Q32 answer and in the worst case (0.85^10 = 0.20) about 2 bits.
4. **#1 has no continuum to vary over.** Plans are k! <= 24 discrete orders (B7:34) and the CSR fetch order is fixed by the format; Euler-Lagrange needs a differentiable functional on a path space. What survives is a cost MODEL -- B7's 3-row table gaining a dependent-miss term (133 ns) and one continuous knob, the blocking bin count, which is swept, not solved. Say so plainly: that is arithmetic, not calculus of variations.
5. **#3 and #9, held to the highest standard, land NARROW.** A random walk step is two dependent misses (`rp[v]`, then `ci[rp[v]+r]`), ~266 ns serial, ~70-100 ns batched by D2's lesson, against 23-45 ns per edge slot for the exact frontier kernel -- so per edge, walks are 2-4x MORE expensive than the exact kernel and win only for LOCAL approximate queries (k-hop ego sampling, personalised PageRank) where the exact answer expands to the graph. The crossover is `walks x hops < slots_reached / 2.7`. No query in W or the gate battery wants an approximate local answer. #9: BFS already stops at the empty frontier and CC at "no change" (`gb_cc.bp:9-10`) -- both are hitting times; the ADOPT item is a convergence test for PR with a tolerance derived from the Q32 truncation floor, and the bound on the stopping time is the deterministic contraction (0.85 lambda_2)^k, not optional stopping.
6. **#12's budget half names a real need and the theorem is counting, not measure theory.** For a JOIN the output size is exact from two `rp` arrays (sum over keys of deg_R x deg_S); for BFS the bitmap frontier makes memory exactly 3 x n/8 bytes independent of hop count (already true, never stated); for SpGEMM / multi-hop the pre-execution bound is `flops = sum_i sum_{k in A_i} deg_B(k)`, O(nnz(A)) from `rp` alone, and the exact nnz is Gustavson's symbolic pass. That rule, with a loud trap against `MemAvailable`, is a B7 planner line -- ADOPT it as counting.
7. **The three numbers every verdict below rests on:** 133 ns per random gather vs 0.67 ns of traffic (the lever is work per miss); 2.86 GB/s per core / 8.6 GB/s on three with zero contention / ~12 GB/s aggregate over seven, with the sequential scan instruction-bound at ~24 % of the memory system; and the branch in the push loop being load-bearing as a filter (E2: branch-free with both line touches 23.4 % SLOWER). Stochastic calculus has no continuous-time noise source to describe here: the timing noise this box has is frequency scaling under the `walt` governor (2054 -> 1651 MHz within a minute, EVALS-RESEARCH:97), which is deterministic, readable from `time_in_state`, and fixed by cycle counters (`security.perf_harden`, ROADMAP C5 note), not by volatility estimators.

9. **Other mathematics that would pay (§15):** Little's law as the quantitative MLP budget (6 misses in flight to match one core's stream, 25 for the SoC); the Q32 truncation floor that any convergence test must sit above (or the loop never stops -- `kalman.bp` and `seigtime.bp` already met this at toy scale); LSM amortised arithmetic for B4's tier sizes before they are coded; the rule of three for what 10^5 clean fuzz seeds actually proves (per-seed failure <= 3 x 10^-5 at 95 %); and lattice fixpoints (Knaster-Tarski) as the termination proof behind every stopping rule in #9 and the reason E1's OR-semiring sharing is exact.

## 1. Method, and the two facts that carry the most weight

Read in full: ROADMAP.md (thesis, TG-DONE, A/B/C/D/E, Measured, especially the corrected DRAM row and rows D2/E1/E2), LANG-DB §0 and §9.2, the four sibling research documents (their verdict vocabulary ALREADY / ADOPT / NARROW / REFUTE is reused), BOX.md, and the code named in the Status line. Two facts settle more of the twelve than any other:

- **Exactness.** `rp[k+1]-rp[k]` is a row length, not an estimate (`gb.bp:376-378` `gb_row_lo/hi/degree`; B7:15 "exact cardinalities"; B7:34 "cost(order) = sum of input rows exact from rp"). A join's output multiplicity is `sum_k deg_R(k) x deg_S(k)`, exact from two `rp` arrays. Every proposal whose payoff is "an unbiased estimate" or "controlled error" is competing with a count.
- **Latency, not bandwidth.** Journal 852: a random gather is 133 ns per element against 0.67 ns of DRAM traffic per 8-byte slot; narrowing the index 64 -> 32 bits moved it 1.06x. Frontier BFS runs 23-45 ns per edge slot (ROADMAP Measured), i.e. it already overlaps 3-6 misses per slot-time; E1's 64-source cell shares every miss 64 ways (6.24x against 64 separate runs, 18.9x with pull disabled); D2's surviving restructuring overlaps eight independent misses instead of three dependent ones (+28.4 % / +25.2 %). Any proposal that models the memory system as a bandwidth bus with a saturation point is modelling the wrong resource.

Where a proposal's mathematics is vocabulary for a mechanism the tree already has, it is called **decorative**; where it yields a bound, an algorithm or a guarantee the engine does not have, it is called **load-bearing**. That split is the last column of the table in §13.

## 2. Proposal 1 -- Calculus of variations for the physical fetch order

**What it needs.** A path space with a continuum of admissible paths, a functional J[path] differentiable in the path, and Euler-Lagrange as its stationarity condition.

**What exists.** A query in B7 is one of k! <= 24 join orders (B7:34) times an access-path choice per table (bucket / zone-map skip / scan, B7:33); the physical order of fetches inside a CSR kernel is fixed by the format (`rp` row start, then the `ci` slice, then `vv` if weighted -- `gen_gb.bp:129-168` `wr_row_body`), and the only free orders are row-range order (sequential by construction) and push vs pull (`gen_gb.bp:344`, `:640`). The cost model B7 has is a 3-row table: scan 1, bucket 20, spgemm-probe 15 ns/row (B7:34). None of this is a continuum.

**Category problem, stated.** Euler-Lagrange over a finite set is exhaustive enumeration, which B7 already does (k <= 4). The "continuous" quantities in a plan are bin counts and chunk sizes: propagation-blocking bin count 2^k sized to the private cache (RESEARCH-LITERATURE §3.3) and C5's chunk length. Those are one-dimensional sweeps over a handful of powers of two, and the cache sizes are unknown on this box (sysfs reports 0, WILD-IDEAS 0.2), so the sweep is the only way to find them anyway.

**What survives.** The cost MODEL, and one correction to it that D2 forces: the 3-row table has no term for dependent misses, and the join probe moved 149 -> 106 ns/row with zero change to the number of bytes, purely by making three dependent misses eight independent ones (journal 1788885563). A cost row should read `ns = a x rows + b x independent_misses + 133 x dependent_miss_depth`, with b measured from the grouped probe. That is arithmetic in a lookup table, and it attaches to B7's planner. It is not calculus of variations, and calling it so would be the fudge this document exists to refuse.

**Verdict: REFUTE the variational framing; NARROW to the cost model.** Row: none new -- a B7 line. Gate: B7's cost table predicts the join twin's grouped vs plain probe within 1.3x on both distributions (the two arms exist in `join_twin_grouped.bp`). Kill: if a 3-term linear model cannot rank grouped above plain on Zipf, the planner needs a measured flavour pick (C5), not a better formula. Cost: zero compiler words, zero dependencies.

## 3. Proposal 2 -- Filtrations: the generation store as F_s ⊆ F_t

**What the store already is.** Append-only bump arena of immutable sealed objects (`store.bp:171-178` `st_alloc`, `:180` `st_seal`); commit = write the OTHER superblock with gen+1 (`:193-203`); a reader takes a whole superblock copy and validates its crc (`:209-226` `st_snapshot`); a version is never mutated, its successor carries a `prev` edge (`mvcc.bp:3-15`; `gb.bp:8-10` field 9); compaction is a Cheney copy into a new file published by rename, the old inode alive under any live mapping (`:355-416`). There is no WAL (RESEARCH-BYOK §5 refuted "abolish the WAL" on exactly this fact) and readers hold no lock (LANG-DB:200-206: "the mapping IS the reader token"). So "zero-cost read isolation with no locks and no WAL" is a true restatement of the design sentence, word for word. As vocabulary, #2 is decorative.

**Where the vocabulary is FALSE as stated, and that is the only place it earns its keep.** "A query started at generation s physically cannot see mutations from generation t." Physically it can. The mapping is MAP_SHARED (`store.bp:146`, and `st_map_ro` `:153-158` is PROT_READ over the same file), so every byte the writer appends above `arena_used_s` is visible in the reader's address space the moment it is written, and the two superblock pages are a mutable two-slot ring: after two further commits the superblock the reader took has been overwritten, and a reader that calls `st_root` or `st_pick` again (`:267`, `:77`) gets generation t. The reader is F_s-measurable by CONVENTION -- it holds its root offset in a register and follows only object-relative refs -- not by physics. That convention is exactly what a wild kernel (B3's OPEN TRAP-82 defect: a stale-ABI kernel dispatched against the pool) or a B5 cross-partition reader can break silently.

**The theorem the name makes available, and what it would gate.** In probability terms the reader's view must be adapted: every read is a function of F_s = the bytes in [1024, arena_used_s) plus the superblock copy taken at s. Adaptedness is checkable by the MMU, and the tool landed this week: C1 step 1 added `emit_sys_mprotect` and `gb_fence_ro/rw` bracket every kernel launch (`gb_run.bp:225-226`, `:326-331`, `:563-568`). The F_s fence is the same call with a different range: in the forked reader child, after `st_snapshot`, `mprotect([round_up_page(arena_used_s), size), PROT_NONE)` and `mprotect(pages 0-1, PROT_NONE)`. Then a read outside F_s is a trap 82, loud, at the exact instruction -- "physically cannot see" becomes true to within one page of slack at the generation boundary. Cost: two mprotect syscalls per snapshot (~10-30 us each under proot) against a 1.04 ms fork floor (ROADMAP B3), i.e. noise; per-process VMAs, so the writer is untouched. What it gates: (i) every read-side gate in std_golden running fenced with zero traps is a PROOF that the read set of every kernel is adapted, which today is asserted; (ii) B5's STM read-set validation for cross-partition transactions (B5 §3) gets a hardware oracle -- a validation bug that lets a reader see t shows up as a trap under the fence; (iii) C3 is what makes the filtration COMPLETE: without the commit chain, F_{s-2} is unreachable to a new reader (the superblock ring has two slots), so "F_s for all s" is a C3 property and C3's gate ("reopen at gen 1 / N/2 / N") is its test.

**Verdict: ALREADY as vocabulary (decorative); NARROW-ADOPT the fence as the one load-bearing consequence.** Row S-FENCE (§14). Gate: all read-side std_golden gates pass under the fence (0 trap-82); a deliberately stale kernel that re-reads the superblock traps 100/100; snapshot latency <= +0.1 ms. Cheapest kill: fence the sgraph2 frontier dispatch (`sgraph2.bp:579-621` `bfs_kernel_child`) by hand -- two calls -- and run phase `p`; if the fold changes or a trap fires on a CORRECT kernel, some read path legitimately crosses the generation boundary and the model is wrong. Cost against constraints: none (library code, existing builtin).

## 4. Proposal 3 -- Brownian motion: random walks instead of exact SpMSpV

**The vocabulary is wrong before the mechanism is examined.** A random walk on a graph is a discrete-time Markov chain with transition matrix P = D^-1 A. Its variance is not t: the deviation of the walk's occupation measure from stationarity after t steps is governed by lambda_2(P)^t, i.e. by the spectral gap, and a walk on a graph with a small gap (two communities, W's bipartite order-event graph, a ring) has "Brownian characteristics" only in the sense that every Markov chain does in a scaling limit nobody here computes. So the controlled-variance claim IS proposal #10's spectral half wearing a diffusion costume, and it is treated there (§11, S-GAP).

**The mechanism, priced on this box.** One walk step from v: `ldr rp[v]` (miss, 133 ns), `ldr rp[v+1]` (same line, free), draw r in [0, deg), `ldr ci[rp[v]+r]` (a second, DEPENDENT miss, 133 ns). Serial cost ~266 ns per step per walker, and the chain is inherently dependent -- the next address is the value just loaded, which is the one shape the OoO window cannot overlap within a walker. Across walkers it can: D2's lesson (unroll by 8, stage through a caller-owned buffer, +28 %) applies verbatim, so a batch of 8-16 walkers should run at ~70-100 ns per step. The exact frontier kernel runs 23-45 ns per edge SLOT (ROADMAP Measured) because its misses are independent across slots and E1 shares them 64 ways. Per edge examined, a walk is therefore 2-4x MORE expensive than the exact kernel. Random walks do not "build fast approximate subgraphs" by being cheaper per edge; they can only win by examining FEWER edges.

**Where fewer edges is real: local queries on a large graph.** An h-hop exact expansion from one vertex on sgraph2 (mean degree 10) touches ~10^h vertices and ~10^(h+1) slots: 3 hops = ~10^4 slots = ~300 us. K walks of h steps cost K x h x ~80 ns: 100 walks x 3 hops = 24 us. The crossover is `K x h < slots_reached / 2.7`, and it favours walks precisely when the exact answer would expand toward the whole graph -- personalised PageRank from one vertex, "sample 100 vertices of the 3-hop ego net". Published form: Monte-Carlo PPR (Fogaras et al. 2005; Avrachenkov et al. 2007), where accuracy goes as 1/sqrt(walks) -- i.e. #4's martingale property is the LEMMA that makes the estimator unbiased. For GLOBAL ranking the arithmetic reverses: Monte-Carlo PageRank needs R walks per vertex of length ~1/(1-0.85) = 6.7, so 1M x 6.7 x R x 80 ns = 0.54 x R seconds against a power iteration at ~10M slots x 10-40 ns = 100-400 ms per iteration; with R = 1 the estimate has ~1 significant figure per vertex, useless for a Q32 rank order, and R = 100 costs 54 s against ~25 exact iterations (§10). Exact wins globally by an order of magnitude.

**On the graphs that exist.** sgraph2's uniform build has no community structure to sample (an Erdos-Renyi-like graph mixes in O(log n) steps) and its skew build is 1 % hubs receiving half of all endpoints (`sgraph2.bp:120-124`): a walk lands on a hub in ~2 steps and stays there, which is the regime where uniform-random sampling of a walk's neighbours is a notoriously biased estimator of anything but hub degree. W's graph is bipartite orders x events with events per order in the single digits; every W query (q1-q4, B8 §3) is an exact fold with a production Rust oracle. There is no query in the tree whose acceptable answer is approximate. The 30M-edge citation graph the proposal is written for is not here.

**Verdict: REFUTE the Brownian framing (decorative for "Markov chain"); NARROW the mechanism to "batched walkers for local approximate queries", with no workload to attach it to today.** If it is ever built: one `gen_gb` op (`walk`: S walkers per batch, unrolled by 8 as `join_twin_grouped.bp` does, an LCG per walker from `rng.bp`), no compiler change. Gate: ns per walker-step <= 100 on sgraph2 (i.e. batching recovers >= 2.5x over the 266 ns serial chain); top-10 PPR from one source overlaps >= 8/10 with the exact power-iteration PPR at <= 0.1x its wall. Cheapest kill: a 40-line unbatched walker loop in a scratch `.bp`; if ns/step is already under 150 the OoO window overlaps walkers on its own and the batching template is unnecessary, and if PPR@10 overlap at 100 walks is under 0.5 the estimator is too noisy for a ranking use at this graph size. Cost: none against zero-dep/one-pass; one more python oracle (a numpy PPR).

## 5. Proposal 4 -- Martingales as unbiased estimators of intermediate result sizes

**The design has no estimate to de-bias.** Enumerated, in this engine's terms:

| intermediate quantity | how the engine knows it | exact? |
|---|---|---|
| rows of table T with key = c (bucket access) | `rp[c+1]-rp[c]` (B7:33; `gb.bp:378`) | exact, O(1) |
| rows in range [a,b] | zone map per block (LANG-DB §9.4) | exact upper bound; exact count after the skip pass |
| output rows of a 2-way join on key k | `sum_k (rp_R[k+1]-rp_R[k]) x (rp_S[k+1]-rp_S[k])` | exact, O(keys), before any probe |
| nnz of a SpGEMM product A x B | flops bound `sum_i sum_{k in A_i} deg_B(k)` from two `rp` arrays; exact count from Gustavson's symbolic pass with an SPA (B3 step 3 mxm template) | bound O(nnz(A)); exact O(flops) |
| frontier size at hop h | the frontier bitmap's popcount after hop h (`gen_gb.bp:284-300` `gbfs_settle`) | exact, and it IS the answer being computed |
| group-by cardinality | `rp` counts of the group key's CSR (B7:15) | exact |

An "unbiased estimator" of any row above is a downgrade from a count to a random variable with the same mean. The proposal's motivating error -- "error accumulation across multi-hop joins" -- is the Selinger-optimiser problem of MULTIPLYING per-hop selectivity estimates (Ioannidis-Christodoulakis 1991: errors grow exponentially in the number of joins). It exists only where per-hop cardinalities are guessed. Here each hop's cost is `flops` from `rp`, and the planner enumerates k! <= 24 orders with exact inputs (B7:34); where B2's Zipf twin ever shows the exact-count planner picking a plan > 2x off, the fix named there is a per-bucket count check, not a sampler (RESEARCH-BYOK §4.1).

**Is there any regime where a martingale beats a count?** Only when the count is too expensive to take: an h-hop expansion whose exact frontier does not fit, or a SpGEMM whose symbolic pass is as costly as the numeric one and the planner needs the size BEFORE choosing an order. The first does not arise here (a bitmap frontier is n/8 bytes at any hop, §13); the second is the case where a sampled estimate of nnz(A x B) from a random subset of rows -- which is a martingale under uniform row sampling -- could inform order choice for k >= 3 matrix products at a cost below the symbolic pass. That is the ONE honest home for #4, it is a lemma for #3's estimator, and it opens only when B7 has a 3-way SpGEMM whose orders differ by more than the symbolic pass costs. Published form: Wander Join (Li, Wu, Yi, Zhang, SIGMOD 2016) -- random walks over the join graph for unbiased aggregate estimation, which wins exactly when the exact join is too big to run. W's joins are two-way and its aggregates have exact money oracles (`money.bp`).

**Verdict: REFUTE.** Nothing to build; the sentence to add to B7's blueprint is "cardinalities are counts, not estimates, at every hop, so no estimator row is ever opened unless a symbolic pass is measured to cost more than the plan choice it informs". Kill for a future reopen: measure `flops` bound vs actual nnz(A x A) on sgraph2 uniform and skew (§14's S-FLOPS does this) -- if the bound is within 2x of actual, even the symbolic pass is unnecessary for planning and a sampler is doubly unnecessary.

## 6. Proposal 5 -- The Itô integral: delta batches as discrete sums over an immutable base

**What the proposal describes is B4, and the edge log that already runs.** sgraph2 phase `l` appends a chunk of 10^4 pairs per batch and rebuilds L0 (`sgraph2.bp:150-228`); B4 replaces that with tail COO (cap 4096 triples, append-only within a version) -> L0 (<= 2^18 edges, rebuilt whole) -> L1 (row-block CoW, eWiseAdd merge at promotion), reads = L1 ⊕ L0 ⊕ tail minus tombstones (B4:23-36). "Accumulating delta batches over an immutable base as discrete sums at the column level" is that design, sentence for sentence; at the ECS/column level it is C2 (columns per type). Decorative.

**The one clause that is false today.** "With no reallocation": today L0 is rebuilt WHOLE every batch (the O(N) stall: 747 ms max, 30 us per logged edge, ROADMAP Measured G8 row; B4:1 names it) -- so the current engine reallocates a full CSR per batch. That stops only when B4 step 1 lands (stall <= 10 ms, B4 §5 step 1). The proposal is describing B4's target state as if it were the present.

**Is there anything the Itô name carries that the design lacks?** The Itô integral's defining property is that the integrand is evaluated at the LEFT endpoint of each increment -- H is non-anticipating. Translated: a batch's delta is computed against the base as it stood BEFORE the batch, never against a partially applied one. That is B4's invariant (1) (B4:40: "an object is written only inside the transaction that allocated it; a shared object is copied before modification") and it is what makes eWiseAdd merges order-independent under an idempotent or commutative ⊕. The Itô isometry E[(∫H dW)^2] = E[∫H^2 dt] has no analogue; there is no noise. So the only content is a test that B4's gate already implies ("folds == oracle after every 10^4 updates", B4:7): apply the same 10^4 updates in two batch orders and require identical folds. Worth one line in B4 §7, not a row.

**Verdict: ALREADY (decorative).** No row. Note for B4's owner: add the two-order fold-identity check as the explicit "non-anticipating" test. Cost: none.

## 7. Proposal 6 -- Itô's formula and quadratic variation for hardware-noise-driven tier switching

**What quadratic variation would compute here.** For a time series of per-chunk costs X_1, X_2, ... the quadratic variation is `sum (X_{i+1} - X_i)^2` -- a running variance of increments. Itô's formula (the second-order term ½ f'' d[W]_t) applies to a smooth function of a diffusion; no kernel here computes a function of a diffusion, so the formula itself has no referent. What is left is "switch tiers on measured volatility".

**Why switching push/pull on volatility is wrong, and what C5 already says.** The push/pull decision is a DATA property: the frontier's density relative to the unvisited set (Beamer's alpha; `gen_gb.bp:344` `nf * 14 > n - reached`, `:640` `nfv * alpha > n - nfull`). Timing volatility is orthogonal to it: a noisy clock on a sparse frontier would flip to pull, which E1 measured as the wrong direction at large S (pull "worth almost nothing once S is large", ROADMAP E1). A switch driven by timing variance FLAPS; C5's design puts the decision on "a cheap statistic gathered in the previous chunk (selectivity, distinct-key count, or ... an actual miss rate)" (C5 §1), all data statistics, and C5's kill line already covers the case where the switch never fires on realistic input (C5 §4). Where timing IS the right statistic -- choosing tier-0 vs the specialised kernel on the last chunk's ns/row (C5 §2, B3's tiers) -- the robust estimator is a median over chunks, not a variance of increments.

**The hardware noise this box actually has is not noise.** `scaling_cur_freq` on core 4 moved 2054 -> 1651 MHz within one minute under the `walt` governor, and `cpufreq/stats/time_in_state` is readable and cumulative (EVALS-RESEARCH:97), so the frequency history of a run is an exact histogram, not a random variable. TLB misses are a deterministic function of the access pattern and the 4 KB page size (D3's whole argument). The measurement discipline already in force -- interleaved medians of 3-15 inside a slot, ratios not absolutes, frequency-invariant cycle counts once `security.perf_harden` is cleared -- is the correct response to a deterministic-but-unread disturbance. Modelling it as W_t would replace a readable counter with an estimator of its variance.

**Verdict: REFUTE.** Nothing survives that C5 does not already hold. Cheapest evidence, zero code: run sgraph2 phase `p` on uniform and skew and log the switch decision per level with `clock_ms` per level; if per-level timing at fixed direction varies by more than the 25 % wall-clock spread the box is known for, there is a disturbance worth naming -- and it will be frequency, visible in `time_in_state`.

## 8. Proposal 7 -- SDEs: drift as the execution trend, diffusion as bus contention, to predict SpMSpV completion

**Re-derived against the corrected numbers.** The proposal's diffusion term is "cache micro-noise and core contention for the memory bus". Measured on this box (journal 852): three A78 streams run 28 / 28 / 28 ms against a solo 28 -- contention ZERO at P = 3 on scans; a gather is latency-bound at 133 ns with 0.67 ns of traffic, so traversal "never approaches" the memory system at any P the box has; the only measured degradation is 28 -> 43 ms on one A78 when the four A55s stream simultaneously (a 1.5x shift at the SoC's ~12 GB/s aggregate) -- a deterministic offset when background work is scheduled on the little cores (ROADMAP B6 note), not a random increment. So sigma has no measured physical source at P <= 3, and at P = 7 the effect is a drift shift, not diffusion.

**What the drift term is, exactly, and why it is not stochastic.** The completion time of one frontier level is `(slots examined) x (ns per slot for the chosen direction)`. Slots examined in push = `sum_{v in frontier} deg(v)` = exact from `rp` and the frontier bitmap BEFORE the level runs (one popcount-driven sum over frontier words, `gen_gb.bp:255-280` `gbfs_push` walks exactly those rows); in pull = the sum of row prefixes scanned, bounded by `sum_{u unvisited} deg(u)` and much less in practice (Beamer's early exit, which E1 measured at 3.36x). ns per slot per direction is a measured constant per graph shape (23-45 ns). The predictor is therefore deterministic and computable in O(|frontier|) per level; it is the quantity Beamer's original criterion (m_f vs m_u / alpha) is built from, and it is what B6's scaling rule needs to decide P per level (B6 §3 "measure the single-core kernel's bytes/s").

**Does a diffusion term measure hardware noise or misspecification?** Misspecification. The residual between `predicted` and `measured` per level would be dominated by (i) the direction choice being wrong for that level (E1: alpha 2 vs 14 differ by up to 4x at S = 1), (ii) cache state carried from the previous level (the `nx`/`vis` bitmaps resident or not), (iii) frequency (deterministic, §7). None of those is a Wiener increment, and fitting sigma to them would produce a number that changes whenever the model improves -- the signature of a misspecification term. On a box whose three cores show zero contention, an SDE's sigma^2 is a confession that the drift is wrong.

**Verdict: REFUTE as stated; NARROW to a deterministic per-level cost predictor.** Attaches to C5 (the per-chunk statistic) and B6 (the per-level P decision). Mechanism: in the generated Beamer program (`gen_gb.bp` op 5/6), before each level compute `m_f = sum deg over frontier words` (one pass over the frontier bitmap, ~30 us at n = 1M) and log `pred = m_f x c_dir`; compare with `clock_ms` per level. Gate: predicted within 1.3x of measured on >= 90 % of levels on uniform AND skew, with `c_dir` fitted once per (graph, direction). Kill: if the residual exceeds 30 % on levels where the direction was the better one, something genuinely unmodelled exists -- then, and only then, name it; the first candidate is frequency, readable from `time_in_state`. Cost: template lines in `gen_gb.bp`, no compiler change.

## 9. Proposal 8 -- Fokker-Planck: evolve a density over vertex degrees

**Degree is discrete, static per generation, and exact in O(n).** `deg(v) = rp[v+1]-rp[v]`; a histogram is one pass over 1M `rp` cells with a counting store -- ~3 ms at 3 ns per sequential element (journal 852's instruction-bound scan rate), 8 KB of output. There is no time variable for a density to evolve in: within a generation the graph is immutable; across generations the histogram is recomputed from the new `rp` in the same 3 ms, or maintained from the edge log in O(batch). Fokker-Planck describes the density of a CONTINUOUS state under drift and diffusion; "vertex degree" has neither. Clusters and anomalies "with vector instructions" over a density are a NEON histogram scan (A9), which is exactly the O(n) pass the proposal wants to avoid.

**On the graphs that exist.** sgraph2 uniform: degrees are Poisson(10) (5M undirected LCG pairs over 1M nodes, `sgraph2.bp:111-131`); max degree ~30. sgraph2 skew: two populations -- 10^4 hubs at ~250 in-slots each and the rest at ~7.5 (`:120-124`, half of all destination endpoints routed to nodes 0..9999). Both are known in closed form from the generator; an "anomaly" in the skew build is the hub set, by construction. W: orders have single-digit event counts. Nothing to discover.

**What came out of thinking about degree distributions, which has nothing to do with Fokker-Planck.** The push/pull switch in the tree tests VERTEX counts (`nf * 14 > n - reached`), while Beamer's published criterion compares EDGE counts (`m_f > m_u / alpha`, with m_f = sum of frontier degrees). E1 found alpha = 2 beats alpha = 14 at every S on the same graph (ROADMAP E1), which is the kind of miscalibration a vertex-count proxy for an edge count produces on a graph whose degree distribution is not flat. The degree histogram (or just `m_f`, one pass over the frontier bitmap as in §8) makes the switch Beamer's own, and on the skew build the two criteria diverge by the hub factor (~30x) whenever hubs enter the frontier. That is a real, cheap experiment; it is S-EDGE in §14.

**Verdict: REFUTE.** Survivor (unrelated to the proposal's mathematics): the edge-count switch. Gate for S-EDGE: frontier BFS ns per edge slot with the edge-count criterion <= 0.9x of the vertex-count criterion at its best alpha on sgraph2 SKEW, and not worse than 1.05x on uniform, fold identical (the fold is direction-independent by construction, `gen_gb.bp:324-330`). Kill: instrument the shipped kernel to log both criteria per level; if they pick the same direction on every level of both builds, the proxy is exact enough and the row closes on that log.

## 10. Proposal 9 -- Stopping times: early termination on an accuracy threshold

**What already stops, and on what.** BFS terminates when the next frontier is empty -- the first hitting time of the empty set (`gen_gb.bp:330-346` `gbfs_frontier`, loop on `cnt`). CC terminates "once a round makes no change" (`gb_cc.bp:9-10`) -- the first hitting time of the fixed point of a monotone min-label map. Both are stopping times in the strict sense (decided by F_t at time t) and both are exact, not approximate: ALREADY. `seigtime.bp` (SS-17) even implements the textbook object -- "number of Hotelling iterations until the iterate ENTERS AN EXACT CYCLE of the power map ... first recurrence at td = 123" (`seigtime.bp:4-8`) -- at n <= 64, as a gate.

**What does NOT stop on accuracy: PageRank.** `gb_pr` runs "exactly 10 iterations" by oracle law (`gb_pr.bp:4-5`), mirroring the python oracle's fixed count; `spectral.bp` likewise ("FIXED iteration count", `:9`). A fixed count is a parity law, not a convergence law. How converged is 10? The textbook bound is ||r_k - r*||_1 <= 2 x 0.85^k = 0.39 at k = 10 (about 2 bits of a Q32 answer); the sharp rate for the component orthogonal to the stationary vector is (0.85 x lambda_2(P))^k, and for a random graph of mean degree 10 lambda_2(P) ~ 2 sqrt(d-1)/d ~ 0.6, so 0.51^10 ~ 1.2 x 10^-3 -- about 10 bits. To reach 2^-24 needs ~25 iterations on the uniform graph (log2(1/0.51) = 0.97 bits per iteration) and ~100 in the worst case (0.23 bits per iteration at rate 0.85). So a stopping rule here would most likely INCREASE the iteration count, and its value is a converged answer, not speed. That is the honest first finding.

**The tolerance has a floor, and the tree has already hit it twice.** In Q32, `contrib = (wgt x r[u]) / 2^32` truncates one unit per source, `incoming[v]` accumulates deg_in(v) of them, and `r[w] = base + (d_fp x incoming) / 2^32` truncates again: per component, per iteration, up to ~(0.85 x deg_in + 1) units of deterministic downward error -- ~10 units on uniform, ~215 on a hub. An ℓ1 stopping test with epsilon below n x 10 units ~ 10^7 units ~ 2.3 x 10^-3 of the total mass can never fire on noise-free convergence alone; what actually happens is what `kalman.bp` (an EXACT fp fixpoint, P1000 == P999, `:8-9`) and `seigtime.bp` (an exact period-30 cycle) observed: the truncated map lands on an exact fixpoint or a short exact cycle. So the correct stopping rule in fixed point is either "epsilon in units, above the derived floor" or "exact fixpoint / cycle detection", both of which the python oracle can mirror bit-for-bit because the arithmetic is deterministic. That is the load-bearing content of #9 and it is arithmetic (§15 item 2), not optional stopping: Doob's theorem bounds E[tau] for a martingale; the PR error is a deterministic contraction and its tau is bounded by the spectral gap outright.

**"Budgeted BFS ... when accumulated accuracy crosses a."** BFS has no accuracy to accumulate; it is exact per level. A hop budget is a loop bound and exists (any caller of `gbfs_frontier` can cap levels). "Accuracy" for BFS would mean a sampled reachability estimate, i.e. #3, with #4 as its lemma; refuted there.

**Verdict: ALREADY for BFS/CC (decorative); NARROW-ADOPT for PageRank.** Row S-PR (§14): `gb_pr` gains a stopping rule `max_w |r_k[w] - r_{k-1}[w]| <= eps_units` with `eps_units` derived from the truncation floor (documented in the driver), mirrored in `gb_lagraph.py`, iteration count reported as a row. Gate: fold == oracle under the rule; measured iterations to eps = 2^-24-of-mass on sgraph2 uniform within +-2 of the count S-GAP predicts from lambda_2 (§11); overhead of the test <= 1 % of an iteration (one n-cell max pass against a 10M-slot gather). Kill: if the rule never fires within 200 iterations on uniform, the floor derivation is wrong or the map cycles -- switch to exact-cycle detection, which is `seigtime.bp`'s method at scale (a 30-entry history of a 64-bit hash of the vector).

## 11. Proposal 10 -- Graph theory: CSR/SpMSpV "at bus bandwidth without pointer chasing", and the spectrum

### 11.1 First half: ALREADY, and the physics sentence is wrong

`selfhost/prelude/gb.bp` is CSR over the store (GbMatrix {n, m, nnz, fmt, ref rp, ref ci, ref vv, ref mask, gen, ref prev}, `gb.bp:8-10`); `gen_gb.bp` generates the semiring kernels (mxv/vxm/dmxv/dvxm over or-and / any-pair / plus-times / min-plus, ops 1-4, `:89-226`; the whole Beamer BFS as op 5, `:228-403`; 64-source BFS as op 6, `:405-722`); traversal IS a matrix-vector product over a semiring, and the LAGraph-style folds BFS/PR/TC/CC/SSSP are gated against python oracles (ROADMAP B3). "Citation graphs of ~500k papers as sparse CSR" would be one more `gb_build_pairs` call (`gb.bp:180`) if the edges existed; they do not (§0 item 8).

The bandwidth claim is contradicted by the box. "SpMSpV runs at the maximum memory bandwidth of the ARM A78 bus without pointer chasing": (i) `x[ci[k]]` IS a pointer chase -- the second load's address is the first load's value -- and the CSR gather is latency-bound at 133 ns per element against 0.67 ns of traffic, 200x above the traffic floor (journal 852); (ii) "the bus" has no single number -- 2.86 GB/s per core streaming, 8.6 on three with zero contention, ~12 aggregate across seven, and even the sequential scan is instruction-bound at ~24 % of the memory system; (iii) every measured traversal win came from misses in flight, not bytes: E1's 64-source cell (6.24x vs 64 runs, 18.9x sharing with pull off), D2's surviving eight-wide restructuring (+28 %), and E2's finding that the per-slot branch is load-bearing as a filter. What CSR buys over a pointer graph is that the misses across slots are INDEPENDENT (LANG-DB §9.2's own table: "misses independent -> 10-20 ns/edge" vs "one dependent miss chain per vertex, ~100 ns/edge"); that is the thesis as the tree states it, and it is correct.

### 11.2 Second half: the spectrum -- what is already there, what is genuinely absent

- lambda_1 and its vector: PageRank is a power iteration on the Google matrix, so `gb_pr` is a spectral method; ALREADY.
- lambda_2, deflation, the gap, the Fiedler vector: `spectral.bp` implements `topk_symmetric_fp` -- "power method + Hotelling deflation over CSR spmv" in Q32 (`:6-14`, `:89`); `sgamma.bp` implements a "spectral-gap switch (gamma) + Fiedler sign bipartition" (`:4-9`); `seigtime.bp` the exact-cycle stopping time of the power map. All three are gated, all three at n <= 64 with preallocated buffers, none over the store's GbMatrix, and LANG-DB:524 filed "eigenvalue / spectral gap" under "metaphor for lookup; robustness tool at best" because it was proposed there as an INDEX. So the spectral half is ALREADY at toy scale and absent at scale.
- What is genuinely absent: lambda_2 over a 1M-node store matrix; spectral ordering (Fiedler) as a relabelling; spectral clustering; and -- the one that matters -- the spectral gap used as a BOUND.

### 11.3 The spectral gap as the one number that ties #3, #9 and #10 together

Three questions, one answer:

| question | the bound | who asks it |
|---|---|---|
| how many PR iterations to eps? | k >= log(1/eps) / log(1 / (0.85 lambda_2(P))) | #9, S-PR |
| how long must a random walk be before its sample is unbiased? | tau_mix(eps) <= log(1/(pi_min eps)) / (1 - lambda_2(P)) | #3's walker length |
| could a spectral ordering beat BFS order for locality? | Cheeger: h(G)^2 / 2 <= 1 - lambda_2 <= 2 h(G); a graph with a small gap HAS a sparse cut a Fiedler ordering finds and BFS order does not | D3's relabel choice |

For sgraph2 uniform lambda_2(P) is ~0.6 by the random-graph bulk edge, so mixing is O(log n) steps, PR needs ~25 iterations to 2^-24, and Cheeger says there is no sparse cut -- BFS order is as good as any (D3's "hot-vertex reorderings can lose" warning does not bite on an expander). For the skew build lambda_2 is larger (hubs make a dense core with a sparse periphery), and the numbers change; measuring them is S-GAP. For W's FSM matrix A^12 == 0 (B8 §3), so Spec(A) = {0}: nilpotent, no gap, nothing to rank -- the spectral half has literally nothing to say about the one matrix W owns.

**Mechanism at scale.** lambda_1 via the existing PR/SpMV loop with `normalize_fp` (`spectral.bp:60`, isqrt in `sgamma.bp`); lambda_2 via one Hotelling deflation step per iteration (`x -= (v1 . x) v1`, an O(n) pass) -- `topk_symmetric_fp`'s method with buffers of n cells instead of 64, over `gb.bp`'s `gb_row_lo/hi/col` accessors; ~200 lines reusing code that already has an oracle shape (`bench/vs_rust/spectral_golden`). Cost per iteration ~ one PR iteration (10M gathers, 100-400 ms at 1M/10M). The Fiedler VECTOR of the Laplacian is harder: power iteration finds the largest eigenvalue, the Fiedler value is the second SMALLEST, and shifted iteration on (lambda_max I - L) converges at a rate set by the gap over lambda_max -- hundreds of SpMVs on a 1M graph in Q32 (tens of seconds), with Lanczos numerically delicate in fixed point. That is affordable once per compaction as a D3 variant and nowhere else.

**Verdict: first half ALREADY (decorative, and the bandwidth claim REFUTED); spectral half NARROW-ADOPT lambda_2 at scale (S-GAP), Fiedler ordering only as a measured D3 variant.** S-GAP gate: lambda_2(P) on sgraph2 uniform and skew within 2^-8 of a numpy power-iteration oracle (no scipy; the oracle stack is python already); the predicted PR iteration count to 2^-24 within +-2 of S-PR's measured count on both builds; wall <= 30 PR-iteration equivalents. Kill: if deflated power iteration in Q32 does not converge to a stable lambda_2 within 200 iterations on the skew build (a near-degenerate pair), the method is Lanczos or nothing, and the row closes with that number. D3 variant gate: frontier ns/slot after Fiedler-order relabel <= 0.9x after BFS-order relabel on the skew build (where a cut exists), else BFS order stays. Cost: library + oracle; no compiler change; no dependency.

## 12. Proposal 11 -- Differential-equation analysis: phase portrait, Lyapunov, bifurcation at 12 GB/s, "linearise into bytecode"

**There is no dynamical system.** A phase portrait, Lyapunov stability and bifurcation are properties of a state that evolves under a vector field. Throughput as a function of core count P is a static curve -- a roofline -- with no time evolution and no equilibrium to be stable about. B6's "stop scaling at the bandwidth ceiling" (B6 §3) is a static rule on that curve. "Model engine behaviour under peak load" here is: measure the curve once per kernel shape and store the knee.

**The knee the proposal names is at a number that was corrected, and it moves in two directions.** Re-derived: scans -- one A78 at 2.86 GB/s, three at 8.6 GB/s with 28/28/28 ms (zero contention), so there is NO saturation at P <= 3 and B6's rule "do not spawn when the single-core kernel is at >= 80 % of 12 GB/s" can never fire for a single core (it reaches 24 %); the only measured knee is at P = 7 with the A55s streaming (28 -> 43 ms, aggregate ~12), i.e. the rule must be re-based on the AGGREGATE and on which cores are loaded, which the DRAM-row correction already instructs (ROADMAP B6 note (a): "3 cores x 4x would need ~34 GB/s and the ceiling is ~12 -- they do not multiply"). Traversals -- latency-bound, "the memory system is never approached" (B6 note (b)), so cores and per-core MLP DO multiply and there is no knee at any P this box has; the ceiling that binds is misses in flight per core (§15 item 1), which is additive across cores. So "bifurcation points where parallel traversals saturate the 12 GB/s memory limit" describes a regime the traversal kernels cannot reach on this SoC. "Auto-stabilise execution" is B6's static rule with the right ceiling; nothing dynamical.

**"Linearise nonlinear graph-query conditions into matrix operations inside bebop bytecode, removing interpretation overhead."** There is no bytecode and no interpreter: bebop compiles `.bp` to arm64 words in one pass (ROADMAP thesis item 1), a query is a compiled function (LANG-DB §4f), and a `where p` predicate is a compare plus `csel` after A2 (ROADMAP A2/A2b). The matrix form of a predicate over a graph is a GraphBLAS `select` or a mask vector -- B3 step 3's `select/apply/mask` templates, ALREADY. "Linearisation" of a nonlinear condition (a Jacobian at an operating point) has no meaning for a boolean predicate; it is either true or false per row. What survives of this half is nothing.

**Verdict: REFUTE both halves.** The one instruction it leaves behind is already in the roadmap: re-base B6's stop rule on the measured per-core (2.86) and aggregate (~12) numbers, and measure the gather-scaling curve for P = 1/3/7 before writing any rule for traversals (WILD-IDEAS 2.3 / B6 step 0). Kill, zero code: B6 step 0's own probe.

## 13. Proposal 12 -- Measure theory: mu(A) as a memory budget; L^p for vectors and similarity

### 13.1 The budget half

The need is real: this box SIGKILLs the 33rd process, MemAvailable is ~2.4 GB (BOX.md), and the honesty floor says every capacity is a loud trap (TG-DONE 4). But the guarantee "intermediate results cannot exceed mobile limits before execution starts" is a COUNT per plan shape, and the counting is uneven:

| shape | bound available before execution | from | exact? |
|---|---|---|---|
| bucket / range access | `rp[c+1]-rp[c]`; zone-map block count x block size | `rp`, zone maps | exact / exact upper |
| 2-way join output | `sum_k deg_R(k) x deg_S(k)` | two `rp` arrays, O(keys) | exact |
| BFS / multi-hop reachability (frontier form) | 3 bitmaps x n/64 cells = 3n/8 bytes at ANY hop; queue form <= n cells | the representation (`gen_gb.bp:330-346`: fr/vis/nx) | exact, hop-independent, and never stated as a guarantee anywhere in the tree |
| 64-source BFS (E1) | 5 x 8n bytes (fr, vis, nx, anx, act as i64 cells) | `gen_gb.bp:582-600` | exact |
| SpGEMM / h-hop as a product A^h x | `flops = sum_i sum_{k in A_i} deg_B(k)` >= nnz(A x B); exact nnz from Gustavson's symbolic pass; SPA = n cells x P workers (B6 §3 already caps: "fall back to P = 1 when n x P x 8 B > 64 MB") | two `rp` arrays, O(nnz(A)) | upper bound; exact after a symbolic pass |
| mxm chain of k matrices | flops bound per product, order-dependent; `nnz(A^h x)` <= min(n, |x| x d_max^h) | `rp`, d_max | loose (d_max ~300 on the skew build) |

So "for a JOIN the bound is already exact" holds; "not exact for multi-hop, transitive closure and BFS frontier growth" is half right: for BFS in the frontier form the memory is a CONSTANT of n, which is a stronger guarantee than any measure, and it costs nothing to state; for products the theorem that pays is the flops bound, computable in one O(nnz) pass, tight to ~1.1x on a random graph (sum deg^2 ~ n(d^2 + d) = 110M vs nnz(A^2) ~ 100M after collisions) and looser on hubs. None of this is measure theory -- sigma-additivity of mu over disjoint row ranges is just "the sum of row lengths is the total", which `rp[n]` already stores. What measure theory would add over counting (measurable spaces, completions, L^p closure) has no object here: every set is finite.

**Verdict: NARROW-ADOPT as counting.** Row S-FLOPS (§14, a B7 planner line): before dispatching any product or multi-hop plan, compute the flops bound from `rp`, add the representation's fixed cost (bitmaps, SPA x P), compare against `MemAvailable` read at open (`/proc/meminfo`, readable here) minus a floor, and refuse with a loud trap code rather than allocate. Gate: bound >= actual nnz on 100 % of products (a violated bound is a bug), bound <= 2x actual on sgraph2 uniform and <= 5x on skew; a plan whose bound exceeds the budget exits with the trap code before `st_alloc`. Kill: zero code -- compute the bound and the actual for `A x A` on sgraph2 with the existing mxm template; if the ratio on uniform is > 3x, the bound is too loose to plan with and the planner must run the symbolic pass instead (still exact, still cheap, still counting).

### 13.2 The L^p half

L^p spaces are for functions on a measure space with p-th powers integrable; on finite i64 vectors they are ℓ^p norms. What exists: ℓ2 normalisation in Q32 (`spectral.bp:60` `normalize_fp` with isqrt), n <= 64; ℓ1 sums are the plus-times reduce (`gen_gb` sr 3); ℓ∞ is one `max` reduce; a (max, |.|) or (+, |.|) semiring instance is one more template line. What the proposal wants them for -- "vector computation, ranking and similarity metrics" -- needs vectors per entity: embeddings. The acceptance workload W is an order log whose aggregates are money sums with exact i64 oracles (`money.bp`, B8 q4); ℓ1 of amounts is a sum, and "similarity between orders" is not a W query. The arXiv corpus has titles and abstracts and no embeddings; producing them would be a model dependency the project forbids at runtime. So L^p answers a workload the project does not have, and its cheapest form (a norm reduce) is one template line whenever a workload arrives.

**Verdict: REFUTE for now.** No row. Reopen trigger: a workload with per-entity vectors and an oracle for the similarity it wants.

## 15. What other mathematics would actually pay here

Given: a latency-bound gather, an instruction-bound scan, exact cardinalities, an append-only arena, no IR, Q32 fixed point, seven cores with a shared ~12 GB/s aggregate and a 32-process cap. Same standard: mechanism, attachment, number, kill.

1. **Little's law (queueing), as the MLP budget -- the most useful single line of mathematics for this engine.** Concurrency = latency x throughput. At 133 ns per miss and 64 B per line, one miss in flight is 0.48 GB/s; matching one core's 2.86 GB/s stream needs ~6 misses in flight, and the SoC's ~12 GB/s aggregate needs ~25 across cores. Read backwards, every measured kernel gets a concurrency number: frontier BFS at 45 ns/slot has ~3 misses in flight, at 23 ns ~6; the plain join probe (149 ns for a 3-deep chain, 399 ns serial) ~2.7, grouped (106 ns) ~3.8. That makes "misses in flight per kernel" a first-class perf row -- the quantity D2, E1 and E2 were all measuring without naming -- and it says how far each kernel is from the per-core window (~a dozen on an A78, LANG-DB §9.2) and what B6's traversal scaling can be (additive in P). Attaches to `docs/PERF.md` rows and B6 step 0. Gate: every traversal kernel reports `mif = 133 / ns_per_dependent_miss`; a new traversal row is accepted only if mif >= 4. Kill: none needed; it is derived from numbers already pinned.

2. **Fixed-point error analysis for Q32, as the floor every convergence test sits above.** Per-op truncation <= 1 unit; per-component per-iteration error <= (0.85 x deg_in + 1) units for PR (§10); the accumulated mass loss over k iterations ~ k x n x 10 units on uniform. This is what decides `eps_units` in S-PR, why `kalman.bp` reaches an EXACT fixpoint and `seigtime.bp` an exact cycle, and why spectral.bp's parity is "few LSBs, not bit-exact" (`:11-12`). Attaches to S-PR and to every Q32 kernel's oracle tolerance. Gate: the derived floor is never exceeded by the measured per-iteration residual on sgraph2. Kill: one measured residual above the floor means an unmodelled truncation site.

3. **LSM amortised arithmetic for B4's tiers, done before any of it is coded.** With tail 4096 triples, L0 <= 2^18 edges, 64-row L1 blocks and a 512-fan-out blocktab (B4:23-34), write amplification and bytes appended per single-row update are closed-form (T x L for a size ratio T over L levels; RESEARCH-BEAT-SQL §2.4 S2 asked for the bytes-per-commit gate and RESEARCH-LITERATURE §9 for a bytes-per-edge gate). Attaches to B4 step 0. Gate: predicted bytes per update within 1.5x of measured after 10^5 updates. Kill: if the analytic number is > 2 KB per update, the tier sizes change before a line of merge code exists.

4. **The rule of three, for what the fuzz lane proves.** Zero CRASH/DIVERGE in N seeds bounds the per-seed failure probability at 3/N with 95 % confidence: 28.5k seeds -> 1.05 x 10^-4; 10^5 -> 3 x 10^-5. Good-Turing on the singleton count of trap/behaviour classes estimates the mass of classes not yet seen. Both are one line in `docs/PERF.md`'s `fuzz_seeds_on_bin` row and turn TG-DONE 8's 10^5 into a stated guarantee. No code; no kill needed.

5. **Lattice fixpoints (Knaster-Tarski) as the termination proof behind #9 and the correctness of E1.** CC's min-label map is monotone on a finite lattice, so it reaches its least fixpoint in <= n rounds -- the stopping rule "no change" is provably terminating and provably exact. E1's (OR, AND) semiring is a boolean lattice join, idempotent, so 64 sources' frontiers can share one cell with no cross-talk -- that is WHY the 6.24x is exact and why the same trick cannot be applied to a non-idempotent semiring (plus-times) without a carry. Decorative for what exists; load-bearing as the rule for which future semirings may be bit-packed: idempotent ⊕ only. Attaches to `gen_gb` op 6's comment block.

6. **Cheeger's inequality and expander mixing, as the criterion for D3.** Whether a spectral relabel can beat BFS order is decided by whether the graph has a sparse cut (h(G) small <=> 1 - lambda_2 small). On an expander it cannot; on a community graph it can. S-GAP produces the number; D3 chooses the ordering from it rather than measuring both blind.

7. **Order statistics on the skew build, for the pull step's worst case.** d_max on the 1 %-hub build is ~300 and on Zipf(1.1) grows like n^(1/1.1); the pull step's cost per level is bounded by sum over unvisited of min(deg, prefix scanned), and the expected prefix scanned for a hub row is 1/(frontier density) -- closed-form, and it is why pull is worth 3.36x at S = 1 and nothing at S = 64 (E1). Attaches to S-EDGE's alpha choice.

8. **What would NOT pay, stated once:** stochastic calculus of any kind (no continuous-time noise source exists on this box; every disturbance found this week was deterministic and readable), PDEs (no fields), dynamical-systems theory (no evolving state), information-theoretic compression bounds beyond the entropy/derived/padding split WILD-IDEAS §4.2 already asked for (the size row is a layout problem, C2/A8), and any probability that needs float: the oracle side may use it, the engine stays i64.
