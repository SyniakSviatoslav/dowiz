Eight complaints, one direction: unclear UI · bad paddings · cramped, especially on
phones · unclear what each control does · too few example placeholders · too few
explanations · too few labels · poor placement. The direction is Apple's Human Interface
Guidelines (HIG), which are written for exactly this: a phone held in one hand by someone
who did not read a manual. This document is the set of rules taken from the HIG, each with
the page it comes from, and how the console applies them. `HUB-UX-2026-09-26.md` (the
previous pass) stands underneath it: one heading per screen, rows not tiles, the desktop
sidebar, the theme choice. This pass is the layer the operator said was missing: the
**form**, the **word next to the control**, and the **room around it**.

Evidence: `scratchpad/shots/before/phone-light/*.png` (66 screens and sheets, 390×844,
taken on this tree at ddf3ecfa before any change) and `scratchpad/shots/after/…` (same
walk, after). Section 6 lists what each shows.

## 1. What the HIG says, and the rule the console takes from it

Sources are Apple's Human Interface Guidelines, read 2026-09-26 at
`developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/<page>`. Quotes are verbatim; the
rule under each quote is this console's.

### 1.1 Typography (`/typography`, "Specifications", iOS Large (default))

| Style | Size / leading (pt) | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Large Title | 34 / 41 | Regular (Bold emphasised) |
| Title 1 | 28 / 34 | Regular |
| Title 2 | 22 / 28 | Regular |
| Title 3 | 20 / 25 | Regular |
| Headline | 17 / 22 | Semibold |
| Body | 17 / 22 | Regular |
| Callout | 16 / 21 | Regular |
| Subhead | 15 / 20 | Regular |
| Footnote | 13 / 18 | Regular |
| Caption 1 | 12 / 16 | Regular |
| Caption 2 | 11 / 13 | Regular |

"**Use font sizes that most people can read easily.**" · "**In general, avoid light font
weights.**" · "**Minimize the number of typefaces you use.**"

**Rule T1 — the console's scale is Apple's, rendered from one ratio.** `lib/tokens.css`
derives every step from `--text-base` at ×1.2. With `--text-base: 1rem` (16 px) the
derived steps land on the table above: `--text-3xl` 33 ≈ Large Title 34, `--text-2xl` 28 =
Title 1, `--text-xl` 23 ≈ Title 2, `--text-lg` 19 ≈ Title 3, base 16 = Callout, `--text-sm`
13 = Footnote, `--text-xs` 11 = Caption 2. The console read at 15 px (Subhead) before; it
reads at 16 px now (the storefront stays at 17, Body). Nothing anyone must read is under
13 px (Footnote) and nothing under 11 px exists.

| Console element | Apple style | Size |
|---|---|---|
| Screen title (`.screen-h h1`) | Title 1 on a phone, Large Title from 1024 px | 28 / 33 |
| Sheet title (`.sheet h2`) | Title 2 | 23 |
| Section heading in a list (`.cat-h`, `.kds-colh`) | Title 3 | 19 |
| Row title, button label, field text | Callout/Body | 16 |
| Field label (`.ui-label`) | Subhead, semibold | 15 |
| Footer explanation (`.ui-hint`, `.hint`, `.screen-hint`) | Footnote | 13 |
| Section header (`.eyebrow` inside a sheet) | Footnote, uppercase, tracked | 13 |
| Row subline, meta | Footnote | 13 |
| Tab bar word | Caption 2 | 11 |

### 1.2 Layout (`/layout`)

"**Order content by relative importance.** … place the most important items near the top
and leading side." · "**Align elements to make them easier to scan, and use indentation to
convey hierarchy.**" · "**Group related items to clearly express related information or
functions.** … you might use negative space, container shapes, or separator lines." ·
"**Use progressive disclosure to make layouts cleaner.**" · "**Be prepared for text-size
changes.** … horizontally adjacent views may need to stack vertically."

**Rule L1 — margins are 16 pt on a compact phone and 20 pt from 400 pt.** Apple's layout
margins on iPhone are 16 pt at the narrow widths and 20 pt on the wider ones; the console's
`.wrap` and `.sheet-in` follow that (`--margin`). Inside a card or row the inset is 16 pt.

**Rule L2 — the spacing scale is 4 pt** (`--space-*`, already in tokens): 4 inside a
control, 8 between a label and its field, 12 between fields of one group, 24 between
groups, 32 before a section that starts a new thought. The 8 px that stood between two
fields before now stands between a label and its own field.

**Rule L3 — on a phone, side-by-side fields stack** unless both are short numbers that
belong together (price · minutes; kcal · protein). A three-column line (the delivery
invoice) becomes two rows. A control never truncates its own label.

**Rule L4 — the most important control is nearest the thumb**: the one primary action of a
sheet is the last thing, full width; a screen's one creating action sits in its heading.

### 1.3 Text fields and entering data (`/text-fields`, `/entering-data`)

"**Show a hint in a text field to help communicate its purpose.** A text field can contain
placeholder text — such as 'Email' or 'Password' — when there's no other text in the field.
Because placeholder text disappears when people start typing, it can also be useful to
include a separate label describing the field." · "**Be clear about the data you need.**
… you might display a prompt in a text field — like 'username@company.com' — or provide
an introductory label that describes the information, like 'Email.' You can also prefill
fields with reasonable default values." · "**When possible, offer choices instead of
requiring text entry.**" · "**Use a number formatter** … accept only numeric values." ·
"**Evenly space multiple text fields.** … Stack multiple text fields vertically when
possible, and use consistent widths." · "**Match the size of a text field to the quantity
of anticipated text.**"

**Rule F1 — every field has a visible label.** A placeholder is never the only label
(it disappears on the first keystroke). `ui.field` already renders `<label for>`; the
console's `parts.field` refuses to draw a control that has neither `key` nor `label`
(the two search boxes and the count sheet's per-row inputs carry `aria-label` and a
visible name beside them).

**Rule F2 — every empty field shows an example, not its name.** The example is a plausible
value in the venue's own world: "p.sh. Fileto salmoni", "p.sh. 1 200", "p.sh. +355 69 123
4567". The words are in `admin/apple-words.js` (`ex_*`), looked up by field id first and
by label key second, so a screen that renders `field({ key: 'phone' })` gets the example
without naming it. A field with a value shows the value; the example is for the empty one.

**Rule F3 — a footer explains what is not obvious, in one line.** Under the field, Footnote
size, muted: what unit ("Në lekë të plota"), what it changes ("Nën këtë shfaqet «pak»"),
what "empty" means ("Bosh = pa kufi"). Same lookup (`foot_*`) by id then key. A field whose
meaning is its label ("Emri") has none.

**Rule F4 — numbers get the numeric keypad** (`inputmode`), and a money field is whole
lek. Choices of five or fewer are chips, not typing.

### 1.4 Lists and tables (`/lists-and-tables`)

"In iOS and iPadOS … the grouped style uses headers, footers, and additional space to
separate groups of data." · "**Keep item text succinct so row content is comfortable to
read.**" · "If you need to let people drill into a list or table row's subviews, use a
disclosure indicator."

**Rule G1 — a settings form is an inset grouped list**: a group name above (Footnote,
uppercase, muted, 16 pt inset), the group as one rounded surface with hairline separators,
a footer below in the same Footnote. This is what iOS Settings, Wallet and Reminders do,
and it is the shape `ui.list({ inset: true })` already draws for rows; the console's
`.sheet-in .eyebrow` is that header and `.hint` after a group is that footer.

**Rule G2 — a row that opens something ends in a chevron; a row that is a fact has none.**
Already so (`rowBtn` vs `rowDiv`); the rule is written so the next screen keeps it.

### 1.5 Toggles (`/toggles`)

"**Use the switch toggle style only in a list row.** You don't need to supply a label in
this situation because the content in the row provides the context." · "**Clearly identify
the setting, view, or content the toggle affects.**" · "**Make sure the visual differences
in a toggle's state are obvious.**"

**Rule S1 — a switch is a list row: the name on the left, the switch on the right, the
one-line consequence under the name.** The console drew its switch on the LEFT with the
word after it; it now draws the word first and the 51×31 switch at the trailing edge (the
iOS switch's own size), on a row of its own with a hairline. A switch whose effect is not
plain from its name carries a footer ("I fikur, klienti nuk e sheh dot pjatën").

### 1.6 Buttons (`/buttons`)

"**In general, use a button that has a prominent visual style for the most likely action in
a view.**" · "**Keep the number of prominent buttons to one or two per view.**" · "consider
starting the label with a verb" · "a primary button uses an app's accent color, whereas a
destructive button uses the system red color."

**Rule B1 — four levels, and the mapping is fixed**: filled = `primary` (one per view,
the accent); tinted = `success` (the green "back on sale" / "ready" — the exception that
must stand out); gray = `secondary` (the default look of every other button); plain =
`ghost` (a row's quiet action, a cancel). `danger` is filled system red and appears only
on the destructive act itself, never on a list's default control.

**Rule B2 — a destructive act is explained before it happens.** `core.confirm()` takes a
`hint`: one sentence on what will change ("Klienti njoftohet menjëherë dhe paratë e
paguara online kthehen"). The sheet reads: what (title), which (body), so what (hint),
then Cancel · Do it. Every destructive call site passes one.

### 1.7 Sheets (`/sheets`)

"**Display only one sheet at a time.**" · "**Provide an alternative to the Done button** …
pair it with a Cancel button." · "**Include a grabber in a resizable sheet.**" · "**Support
swiping to dismiss a sheet.**"

**Rule H1 — a sheet says in one sentence what it is for** under its title, Footnote,
muted, before the first control: "Emri, telefoni dhe adresa që sheh klienti." Every sheet
has one; a screen has one under its title (`.screen-hint`). The breadcrumb line above the
title (the group name) stays: on a phone the sheet covers the screen and it is the only
"where am I".

**Rule H2 — × and swipe close; the primary action saves.** Already so; kept.

### 1.8 Accessibility (`/accessibility`)

| Text size | Weight | Minimum contrast |
|---|---|---|
| Up to 17 pt | All | 4.5:1 |
| 18 pt | All | 3:1 |
| All | Bold | 3:1 |

"**Offer sufficiently sized controls.**" iOS default control size **44×44 pt** (minimum 28).

**Rule A1 — 44 pt targets, AA contrast, colour is never the only signal.** The tap-size
gate and `lib/ui/tokens.test.mjs` already measure both; the switch grows to 51×31 inside a
44 pt row; a status is a dot AND a word.

### 1.9 How Apple's own apps do a settings form (what was looked at)

iOS Settings › Wi-Fi › "Other…": a grouped list, "Name" on the left and the placeholder
"Network Name" in the field; the section header "SECURITY" in uppercase Footnote; a
footer sentence under the group. Wallet › Add Card: one field per row, examples in every
field, one filled button at the bottom. Reminders › New List: the name field first with
the example, choices (colour, icon) as a grid of chips, nothing typed that can be picked.
The three share: label + example + footer, one primary action, choices over typing.

## 2. Copy rules (every screen, every sheet, every field)

1. **Every screen says in one sentence what it is for**, under its title (`.screen-hint`).
2. **Every sheet says in one sentence what it is for**, under its title (`.sheet-hint`).
3. **Every field has a visible label.** Never a placeholder alone.
4. **Every empty field shows an example** ("p.sh. …" / "e.g. …" / "напр. …").
5. **Every field whose meaning is not its label has a one-line footer.**
6. **Every switch names its consequence** when the name alone does not.
7. **Every destructive action is explained** in the confirmation (what changes, for whom).
8. **Units are in the label or the footer**, never assumed: lek, g, ml, %, min, days.
9. **Words are the surface's**: i18n keys, `data-t`; new words in `admin/apple-words.js`,
   sq/en/uk, and the merge adds a fourth language without a code change.
10. **Behaviour is untouched.** Ids, `data-*`, `data-tour` anchors and handlers stay; a
    lookup that finds no example or footer renders exactly what rendered before.
