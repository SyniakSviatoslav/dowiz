## 1. The ordering principle

Three rules decide the order of everything below, and they are the reason it is not sorted by
excitement:

1. **A room cannot open without the room.** A waiter who cannot exist, a bill that cannot be
   amended and a table that cannot hold a tab are not features of a POS; they are the POS. Nothing
   downstream of them is worth building first.
2. **A wrong number is worse than a missing one.** Tax is absent from the order path entirely.
   Adding channels and prices on top of that multiplies a wrong figure across three channels
   instead of one, so tax comes before pricing and both come before fiscalisation.
3. **A record you may not use is not a record.** A customer profile without a consent record cannot
   lawfully be sent anything, so CRM is last — not because it matters least, but because building
   it earlier produces something that has to sit unused.

## 4. What is recommended AGAINST, and when to re-open it

A roadmap that recommends everything is a wish list. These are decided, and each says what would
change the decision.

- **CRDTs for money and orders** — `DECISIONS.md:369` fences them out; a Durable Object is already
  a single writer per venue, and `queue + Idempotency-Key + a refusing FSM` is a deterministic
  merge by construction. **Re-entry:** a second writer that cannot share an object — *an offline
  POS till*. **That condition may now be met**, and the stack lane has been told to re-examine it
  rather than inherit the answer.
- **A general effect system in Rust** — the cost is rewriting 519 + 185 awaits over `!Send` wasm
  futures for a property the kernel and hub already have by construction. Injecting the clock
  bought most of the rest, and that is now done (93 → 0). **Re-entry:** a second runtime target
  that cannot link the kernel directly.
- **A general event bus between in-process services** — `BLUEPRINT-MODULAR-ARCHITECTURE` §9: "a
  direct typed call is debuggable; a bus is a distributed system with none of the benefits."
